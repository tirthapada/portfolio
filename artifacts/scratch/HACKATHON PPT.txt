Lunar AI: Intelligent Landing Site Selection & Rover Navigation
An AI-powered system that analyzes lunar satellite imagery, detects terrain hazards, recommends the safest landing zones, and plans optimal rover paths — built for the next era of Moon and Mars exploration.

--- ppt/slides/slide4.xml ---
Why Landing on the Moon Is Harder Than It Looks
Terrain Hazards
Craters, boulders, steep slopes, and regolith make manual landing site selection extremely risky. A single misjudgment can destroy a mission.
Manual Analysis Is Too Slow
Human experts analyzing satellite imagery take hours or days — far too slow for autonomous or time-critical missions.
An AI-assisted, real-time system is essential for safe, scalable lunar exploration.

The Moon Is Back — And the World Is Racing
Why This Matters Now
NASA's Artemis Program and ISRO's Chandrayaan missions have reignited global lunar exploration. Safe, autonomous landing and navigation are no longer optional — they are mission-critical requirements.
Artemis III targets crewed south pole landing by 2026
ISRO planning Chandrayaan-4 sample return mission

End-to-End AI Pipeline: From Image to Decision
Every stage feeds the next — raw satellite imagery is transformed through AI processing, terrain analysis, and hazard detection into a ranked landing recommendation with a quantified safety score and an optimal rover path, all visualized on an interactive mission dashboard.

--- ppt/slides/slide8.xml ---
Five Core Modules
Terrain
Analysis
Classifies surface types — flat, sloped, rough — using elevation and texture data
Hazard Detection
YOLO-based detection of craters, boulders, and dangerous terrain features
Landing Recommendation
Scores and ranks candidate zones, recommending the safest with explainability
Rover Navigation
A* algorithm computes obstacle-free, energy-efficient traversal paths
Dashboard Visualization
Real-time interactive mission dashboard with hazard maps, scores, and reports

--- ppt/slides/slide9.xml ---
System Architecture
Architecture Overview
The system follows a clean four-layer architecture — from raw data ingestion through AI processing and decision-making to an interactive output layer — ensuring modularity, scalability, and real-time performance.
Input Layer:
NASA lunar datasets + user-uploaded imagery
Processing Layer:
OpenCV preprocessing, YOLO detection, TensorFlow classification
Decision Layer:
Terrain scoring, hazard mapping, A* path planning
Output Layer:
Dashboard, safety reports, heat maps, mission summaries
